# How the IETF works

**TL;DR:** The IETF develops specifications such as OAuth and SCIM through chartered working
groups with chairs and an Area Director. Work usually begins as an Internet-Draft. A working
group may adopt it, revise it, run a working group last call, and submit it for Area Director
and IETF-wide review. The IESG may approve it for publication, after which the RFC Editor
prepares the RFC, but drafts can also stall or expire at any stage. Until publication, cite the
draft revision and recheck it before implementation. Datatracker shows whether a document is
active, under IESG review, or waiting in the RFC Editor queue.

Working groups, Internet-Drafts, and the path to RFC. On 2026-09-16 the OAuth WG listed 36
RFCs, 11 active WG drafts, and 5 drafts with the IESG; of the ones worth knowing,
Transaction Tokens remains an active WG draft and Identity Chaining is in the RFC Editor
queue (→
[08](../08-delegation-and-impersonation/call-chains-transaction-tokens-identity-chaining.md)).
