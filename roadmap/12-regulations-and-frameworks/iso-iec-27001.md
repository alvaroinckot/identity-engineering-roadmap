# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 & the Annex A identity controls

**TL;DR:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is the standard that "defines requirements an ISMS must meet": an
information security management system, a documented process by which an organization applies
risk management to preserve the confidentiality, integrity and availability of its information.
It is the certifiable half of a pair: organizations may seek 27001 certification from a
conformity assessment body, while its companion ISO/IEC 27002:2022 "provides the detailed best
practices and controls that can be applied within the ISMS" and "cannot be certified to".
Identity engineering sits inside the ISMS because access control is one of the control areas
27002 names. Both documents are paid publications, so only their public catalogue and preview
pages are cited here. Each is a third edition, published in 2022.

27001 is written for "companies of any size and from all sectors"; it requires the organization
to apply a risk-management process, and certification is optional ("companies implementing
ISO/IEC 27001 can decide whether they want to go through a certification process"). The
requirements proper are Clauses 4 to 10, and excluding any of them "is not acceptable when an
organization claims conformity"; the third edition aligned the text "with the harmonized
structure for management system standards and ISO/IEC 27002:2022", the document its
bibliography names for the controls themselves, whose catalogue page lists "access control,
cryptography, human resource security, and incident response" among its areas. The cited
catalogue and preview pages expose no Annex A control titles, so this page quotes none. Agentic
control catalogs map their agent controls onto Annex A controls in their own wording — one
reading of how an AI agent enters ISMS scope — and are compared in [Agentic control catalogs:
ATC, CSA AICM & AIUC-1](agentic-control-catalogs.md).

_Not legal advice. Obligations and dates are quoted as published on 2026-09-17; check the
official text before relying on them. The standard itself is paywalled: only ISO's free
catalogue and preview pages are cited._
