# GDPR: identity data as personal data

**TL;DR:** The General Data Protection Regulation applies to any controller or processor
established in the EU, whether or not the processing happens in the EU. Personal data is any
information relating to an identified or identifiable person, including online identifiers, so
user IDs, session identifiers and device identifiers can be personal data when they relate to
such a person. The regulation requires collecting only what is necessary, building data
protection into design and defaults, securing processing with measures appropriate to the risk,
notifying a breach within 72 hours where feasible unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to
people, and, subject to Art. 22(2)–(4), giving people the right not to be subject to decisions
based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effects.

Art. 4(1) defines personal data as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable
natural person", naming "an online identifier" among the identifiers, and Art. 4(5) defines
pseudonymisation as processing after which data "can no longer be attributed to a specific data
subject without the use of additional information", provided that the additional information is
kept separately and is subject to technical and organisational measures ensuring
non-attribution — the legal frame for [pairwise identifiers and data
minimization](../01-primitives/pairwise-identifiers-data-minimization.md). Art. 3(1) makes the
regulation apply to controllers and processors established in the EU "regardless of whether the
processing takes place in the Union or not"; Art. 5(1)(c) requires data to be "limited to what
is necessary"; Art. 25 requires data protection by design and by default; Art. 32(1) requires
security "appropriate to the risk", "including inter alia as appropriate" pseudonymisation,
encryption and "ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience"; Art. 33(1)
requires breach notification "not later than 72 hours" where feasible, unless the breach "is
unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons". Subject to Art.
22(2)–(4), Art. 22(1) covers decisions "based solely on automated processing" that produce
legal or similarly significant effects.
