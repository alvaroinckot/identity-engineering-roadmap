# ISO/IEC 42001:2023: AI management systems

**TL;DR:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023 "specifies requirements for establishing, implementing,
maintaining, and continually improving an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS)". An
AIMS is the set of policies, objectives and processes an organization runs for "the responsible
development, provision or use of AI systems". It is a management system standard built on the
Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle; the preview discusses scope, risk-based selection of controls and a
statement of applicability, and defines audit. ISO does not certify anyone; certification is
voluntary and performed by independent certification bodies. The standard "does not replace
laws or regulations". First edition, published December 2023, 51 pages; the text is paid, so
only ISO's public catalogue, preview and explainer pages are cited here.

The standard applies to organizations that develop, provide or use AI-based products or
services; conformity is voluntary, it "does not replace laws or regulations", and ISO sells it
bundled with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 as an "AI and information security management package". It
shares "the harmonized structure (identical clause numbers, clause titles, text and common
terms and core definitions)" with other management system standards, which ISO says facilitates
consistent implementation alongside them. For an identity engineer the relevant parts are the
controls: a control is a "measure that maintains and/or modifies risk", the statement of
applicability documents "all necessary controls and justification for inclusion or exclusion",
and an organization "may not require all controls listed in Annex A or may even exceed the
list". The Annex A titles are paid text and are not quoted here. Catalogs that operationalize
42001 for AI systems and agents map their own controls onto those Annex A controls; they are
compared in [Agentic control catalogs: ATC, CSA AICM & AIUC-1](agentic-control-catalogs.md).

_Not legal advice. Obligations and dates are quoted as published on 2026-09-17; check the
official text before relying on them. The standard itself is paywalled: only ISO's free
catalogue, preview and explainer pages are cited._
